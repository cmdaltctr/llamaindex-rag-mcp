# Section 1983 (42 U.S.C. § 1983) — short summary

**42 U.S.C. § 1983** is the federal civil-rights statute that allows a private
person to sue a state or local government official, in federal court, for the
deprivation of a right secured by the Constitution or federal law. It is the
primary vehicle for civil-rights litigation against state actors in the United
States.

## Text in plain language

**42 U.S.C. § 1983** creates liability for any person who, acting under colour
of state law, deprives another of a federally protected right. The defendant
must (1) act under colour of state law, and (2) cause the deprivation. The
plaintiff must show both elements; the absence of either defeats the claim.

## What **42 U.S.C. § 1983** does and does not cover

| Covered                                            | Not covered                                  |
| -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Police misconduct by state or local officers       | Federal officer misconduct (use *Bivens*)    |
| Excessive force by jail / prison staff             | Purely private actors with no state nexus    |
| First Amendment retaliation by school officials    | Breach-of-contract claims against the state  |
| Equal-protection claims against municipalities     | Claims that arise solely under state law     |

## Damages and immunity

A successful plaintiff under **42 U.S.C. § 1983** may recover compensatory
damages, punitive damages against individuals (not municipalities), nominal
damages where rights were violated without provable harm, and attorney's
fees under **42 U.S.C. § 1988**. Individual defendants frequently raise
qualified immunity, which shields officials whose conduct did not violate
clearly established law that a reasonable officer would have known.

## Procedural posture

**42 U.S.C. § 1983** suits are typically filed in federal district court
under federal-question jurisdiction. State courts have concurrent
jurisdiction but are rarely chosen by plaintiffs because of perceived
hostility to civil-rights claims and unfavourable discovery rules.

## Citations to read alongside

- *Monroe v. Pape*, 365 U.S. 167 (1961) — established that "under colour of"
  reaches misuse of authority, not only authorised acts.
- *Monell v. Dep't of Soc. Servs.*, 436 U.S. 658 (1978) — municipalities are
  "persons" under **42 U.S.C. § 1983** but only liable for policies or customs.
- *Pearson v. Callahan*, 555 U.S. 223 (2009) — qualified-immunity analysis.
